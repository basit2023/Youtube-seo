Check whether your thumbnail earns the right click

Review the thumbnail and title as one package. A useful preview should be understandable quickly, visually clear, and honest about what happens after the click. Free to use in your browser, with no signup or installation.

Use it with purpose

A practical workflow, not a ranking promise.

Design for a small feed

Creators often evaluate thumbnails at full design size, while viewers meet them inside a crowded feed. Reduce the preview and check whether the main subject, contrast, and emotional direction survive. Short overlay wording usually travels better than a sentence, especially when the title already explains the topic.

Avoid repeating the title

Thumbnail copy should add a useful second layer: a result, tension, comparison, number, or visual proof. Repeating the complete title wastes limited space. Read the image and title together, then remove any word or element that does not make the combined promise clearer.

Protect trust and retention

A misleading image may win a click and immediately lose the viewer. That weakens satisfaction and makes future packaging harder to trust. Use real outcomes, relevant visuals, and expressions that fit the video. The opening seconds should quickly confirm that the title and thumbnail represented the content fairly.
