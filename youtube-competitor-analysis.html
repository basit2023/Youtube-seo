Compare YouTube metadata without copying creators

Use a successful video as evidence of audience language, not as a template to duplicate. Compare coverage, find missing questions, and build a more useful original angle. Free to use in your browser, with no signup or installation.

Use it with purpose

A practical workflow, not a ranking promise.

Study patterns instead of sentences

Competitor research is most useful when it reveals recurring questions, formats, examples, and audience language. Copying a title or description weakens differentiation and can create intellectual-property problems. Record the pattern you observed, then decide how your experience, evidence, or teaching approach can add something genuinely new.

Compare the complete promise

A title alone does not explain why a video works. Review the thumbnail, opening, structure, comments, age, channel context, and visible viewer response. Use metadata comparison to spot missing concepts, but watch the video before concluding that a phrase caused its performance. Correlation is not a ranking formula.

Turn gaps into better research

A missing term is not automatically a keyword to insert. Ask whether it represents a question your audience needs answered and whether your video covers it accurately. If yes, strengthen the explanation. If not, leave it out and protect the focus of the video you actually made.
